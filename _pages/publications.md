### Later Life Social Interactions in Community Spaces

**Status:** Forthcoming in *Social Forces*

**Authors:** Maleah Fekete, Tianyao Qu, Brea Perry, Siyun Peng, and Adam Jindra

**Summary**

This study uses ecological momentary assessment data from older adults to examine how residential population density shapes everyday social interactions, including time spent alone, with family, friends, and others.

### Beyond "Deaths of Despair": Narratives of Distress and Risk-Taking Behaviors Among Rural Working-Class Men

**Published in:** *Rural Sociology* (2026)

**Authors:** Maleah Fekete

**Summary**

Drawing on in-depth interviews with rural working-class men, this study examines how men interpret experiences of psychological distress and how these interpretations are shaped by changing social and economic conditions. It shows that men's distress is often rooted in interpersonal relationships and unmet expectations surrounding masculinity, helping explain how broader structural changes become translated into risk-taking behaviors.

[Read the article](https://doi.org/10.1111/ruso.70037)

### Avoiding Strong Ties: Concealment and Confiding in Close Relationships

*American Sociological Review* (2024)

**Authors:** Mario L. Small, Kristina Brant, and Maleah Fekete

**Summary**

Using nationally representative survey data, this study examines how people respond to personal difficulties within their closest relationships. Contrary to conventional wisdom, we find that adults are as likely to avoid close friends and family as they are to confide in them. We argue that the tension between concealment and disclosure is a fundamental feature of close relationships, suggesting that social isolation may stem not only from lacking close ties but also from repeatedly avoiding them.

**Links**

[Read the article](https://doi.org/10.1177/00031224241263602)

### Updating a Social Cognitive Model of PTSD: Implications for Cross-Group PTSD Research

*Current Psychology* (2024)

**Authors:** Maleah Fekete

**Summary**

This article extends a prior social cognitive model of PTSD by integrating recent research on mentalizing and the role of social context. It argues that PTSD risk may be influenced not only by caregiving relationships, but also by broader developmental social environments, including the trustworthiness of social context.

[Read the article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-024-05707-0)

### Confluent Love and the Evolution of Ideal Intimacy: Romance Reading in 1980 and 2016

*Journal of Popular Romance Studies* (2022)

**Authors:** Maleah Fekete

**Summary**

Using interviews and surveys of romance readers in 1980 and 2016, this study examines how changing gender norms have shaped women's ideals of intimacy in romantic relationships. It finds that while contemporary readers increasingly value women's sexual agency, ideals of emotional intimacy continue to reflect traditional gender expectations, highlighting both change and continuity in the cultural meaning of heterosexual relationships.
